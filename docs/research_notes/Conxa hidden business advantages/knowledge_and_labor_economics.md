# Knowledge loss, undocumented processes, swivel-chair work, and labor economics (CONXA addressable problems)

Method note: ~21 web searches (snippet-level; few primary PDFs opened). Many figures reach me only through secondary or vendor pages; each is flagged. Dated figures are marked (OLD). No figure here was invented; unfound items are in Gaps.

## 1. Cost of knowledge loss / tribal knowledge / retirement waves

### Takeaway
The most-cited numbers (Panopto $47M per large firm per year, IDC $31.5B for the Fortune 500) are survey-based or very old and recycled, so use them as directional only. Per-head replacement cost (50-200% of salary) and 3-12 month ramp-up are widely repeated but weakly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Panopto/YouGov (2018, OLD): US knowledge workers waste 5.3 hours/week waiting for colleagues' information or recreating existing institutional knowledge. Press release headline: $42.5M productivity loss + $4.5M inefficient onboarding = $47M/year for a large US firm. Survey of 1,001 US adults at 200+ employee orgs. Panopto's own ebook page shows a different split ($40.6M + $4.2M = $44.7M). Model uses survey-reported hours, not measured loss. — [Panopto press page](https://panopto.com/?p=2088); [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/inefficient-knowledge-sharing-costs-large-us-businesses-47m-a-year/527892/); [Panopto ebook](https://www.panopto.com/ebooks/valuing-workplace-knowledge/)
- IDC figure: Fortune 500 lose at least $31.5B/year by failing to share knowledge. Traced to IDC via a 2004-era SHRM piece; academic sources attribute to Babcock (2004). OLD and recycled; no primary IDC report found. — [SHRM HR Magazine](https://shrm.org/hr-today/news/hr-magazine/Pages/0504covstory.aspx); [Nuclino summary](https://blog.nuclino.com/not-sharing-knowledge-costs-fortune-500-companies-31-5-billion-a-year)
- Replacement cost: widely repeated range of 50%-200% of annual salary, attributed variously to SHRM or Gallup; I could not verify it on a primary SHRM/Gallup page. SHRM cost-per-hire reported ~$4,683. — [Manatal summary](https://www.manatal.com/blog/cost-of-replacing-an-employee); [Forma](https://joinforma.com/resources/employee-replacement-costs)
- Time to productivity: roughly 3-12 months by role; one source cites Gallup 2024 median 8.2 months for mid-level professionals (secondary, unverified); 59% of businesses say up to 6 months to impact. — [Stealth Agents compilation](https://stealthagents.com/research/time-to-productivity-new-hire-statistics-2026); [Deel glossary](https://deel.com/glossary/time-to-productivity)
- Peak 65: Alliance for Lifetime Income "Peak 65 Zone" 2024-2027 (about 4M Americans turning 65 per year). Utilities: >148,000 utility employees aged 55-64 and 26,000 over 65 (T&D World/trade summary, secondary). A 2006 DOE-era deck said utility executives expected half of the technical workforce to reach retirement age in 5-10 years (OLD). — [Peak 65 coverage](https://www.cfstaffing.com/?p=6233); [T&D World](https://www.tdworld.com/utility-business/article/55338186/addressing-the-utility-workforce-crisis-strategies-for-modernization-and-resilience); [PSERC 2006 (OLD)](https://documents.pserc.wisc.edu/documents/publications/papers/2005_general_publications/Strategies-to-Address-the-Problem-of-Exiting-Expertise_Jan-2006.pdf)
- Accounting pipeline: first-time CPA exam candidates fell from ~48,000 (2016) to ~32,000 (2021), nearly one-third; over 42% of firms reported turning away work due to staffing (CPA Trendlines, secondary). — [NYSSCPA](https://nysscpa.org/news/1051685-how-ai-is-helping-firms-address-the-pipeline-shortage-2025-05-13); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/opinion/the-decline-of-the-accounting-major-a-call-to-action)
- Federal/utility succession-planning audits exist (TVA OIG, other OIG report) documenting knowledge-retention weakness; I did not extract figures. — [TVA OIG](https://www.tvaoig.gov/reports/audit/succession-planning)

### Inferences
- Tribal knowledge is most valuable to capture where it is procedural and screen-based (click-paths in portals), which is CONXA's niche; it does not capture judgment/tacit expertise.
- Using Panopto's 5.3 h/week x a $40-60/h loaded rate gives roughly $11-16k per knowledge worker per year (my arithmetic, survey-based, upper-bound).
- A pitch of "record the retiring expert doing the portal task once" ties to a 50-200% replacement cost and 3-12 month ramp, but the exact percentages need verification before use in customer-facing material.

### Gaps
- APQC knowledge-loss figures, Deloitte retirement surveys, and a verified "25% of utility workforce eligible to retire in 5 years" number were not found.
- No primary SHRM/Gallup replacement-cost source; no primary IDC report.
- Government back-office retirement-eligibility data (e.g., OPM/state agencies) and insurance/manufacturing back-office data not found.

## 2. State of SOP/process documentation; documented vs executed

### Takeaway
Process-capture tooling is a validated market (Scribe $1.3B valuation) but these tools produce static step guides, not executable procedures; I found no hard stats on SOP staleness or authoring cost.

### Cited Findings
- Scribe raised a $75M Series C at $1.3B valuation (Nov 2025, led by StepStone); reported total funding ~$122-135M (sources differ); usage claims 5-6M users and 10M+ tasks documented (sources differ). — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/?p=720328); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/scribe-75-million-series-c-at-1-3-billion-valuation-raised-for-workflow-ai-platform); [PremierAlts](https://www.premieralts.com/companies/scribe/valuation)
- Tango relaunched at tango.ai in April 2025 with a shift toward AI browser automation (vendor comparison blog; weak). — [Supered comparison](https://www.supered.io/blog/tango-vs-scribe/)
- Asana "Anatomy of Work": ~60% of time spent on "work about work" (2019/2021, OLD, Asana-commissioned); 59% in 2022 for medium/large firms; 63% in 5,000+ employee orgs; 2023 knowledge workers estimated 4.9 h/week saved with better processes. — [Business Wire 2019](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20191017005053/en/Asana-Anatomy-Work-Index-Reveals-Employees-Spend); [Business Wire 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210114005374/en/Asana-Anatomy-of-Work-Index-2021-Work-About-Work-Is-Dominating-in-a-Distributed-World); [MarTech Series 2022](https://martechseries.com/analytics/asana-anatomy-of-work-index-2022-work-about-work-hampering-organizational-agility/); [HR Tech Cube 2023](https://hrtechcube.com/?p=38125)

### Inferences
- Scribe's valuation shows buyers pay to capture procedures; the unmet step is turning captured procedures into something that runs, which is CONXA's differentiator.

### Gaps
- No found statistic on % of SOPs outdated, SOP authoring/maintenance cost, Trainual/Whatfix/WalkMe market size, or the documented-vs-executed gap. Needs primary research (APQC, Gartner DAP reports).

## 3. Swivel-chair / manual data-entry work and error rates

### Takeaway
Direct, credible measurements are scarce; most error-rate and hours-lost figures are vendor-sourced. McKinsey's technical-automation potential for data work is the best-sourced anchor.

### Cited Findings
- McKinsey (June 2023, global gen-AI report): technical automation potential of processing data 73% without gen AI, 90.5% with; collecting data 68% without, 79% with (as relayed by secondary summary; verify in McKinsey PDF). US report (July 2023): 21.5% of US work hours automatable without gen AI, 29.5% with, by 2030. — [McKinsey Week in Charts](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/week-in-charts/automatic-for-the-people); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/accelerated-adoption-ai-automate-30-americans-work-hours-mckinsey)
- Manual keying error rates: ~1-4% field level in vendor sources; 0.5-1% skilled/controlled; one source cites 2015 clinical data-entry study at 3.7%; AP invoices 1.6-2%. Cost to resolve one invoice error: $53.50 (IOFM, quoted secondhand), $50-150 broader range. All vendor blogs. — [Lido](https://lido.app/blog/data-entry-error-rates); [ImageToTable benchmarks](https://imagetotable.ai/references/manual-data-entry-error-rate)
- Payroll: EY work, as summarized by a vendor, says ~20% of non-automated payrolls contain at least one error needing correction; EY cost per manual HR data entry $4.78 (Paycom-reported). — [Paycom](https://paycom.com/blog/hr-myths-employees-will-enter-their-information-wrong)
- Pega 2018 desktop study (OLD): 845 keying errors per employee per day, about one per 14 keystrokes (vendor study; implausibly high, treat with caution). — [Pega](https://www.pega.com/ja/node/66496)
- Exalate (integration vendor): up to 15 h/week, 780 h/year, lost to manually updating records across systems. Vendor claim. — [Exalate via press release](https://business.ricentral.com/ricentral/article/accwirecq-2025-10-30-the-integration-tax-manual-data-transfers-cost-enterprises-780-hours-per-team-annually)
- Microsoft Work Trend Index 2025: interruption every ~2 minutes; 117 emails and 153 Teams messages per employee per day; 48% of employees and 52% of leaders call work chaotic. — [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/de-ch/2025/06/17/new-microsoft-study-reveals-the-rise-of-the-infinite-workday-40-of-employees-check-email-before-6-a-m-evening-meetings-up-16/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/microsoft-remote-work-meetings)
- RPA fragility (the incumbent answer): maintenance often cited at 15-20% of build cost per year; Forrester "up to 60% of program cost" (unverified); 45% of firms see weekly bot breakdowns from app changes; Pega survey of 509 decision makers found maintenance harder than expected. All vendor/secondary. — [CustomerThink on Pega survey](https://customerthink.com/survey-most-businesses-find-rpa-effective-but-hard-to-deploy-and-maintain/); [CloudCruise](https://cloudcruise.com/blog/true-cost-rpa-maintenance-why-teams-need-engineers); [Ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/replace-rpa-computer-use-agents-cost-architecture-2026/)

### Inferences
- Even at conservative 1% field error and ~$50 per resolved error, a 10,000-record/month manual process implies ~100 errors, ~$5k/month (my arithmetic).
- RPA's maintenance burden is the labor-economics reason work stays with humans: CONXA's self-healing claim targets exactly this cost line, but the RPA maintenance statistics need primary validation.

### Gaps
- No Gartner hours/week swivel-chair figure; no Salesforce/Slack data; no peer-reviewed error-rate study beyond secondhand citations. Asana/Microsoft "work about work" includes meetings and email, so it overstates repetitive digital tasks.

## 4. BPO / offshore economics and BPO response to AI agents

### Takeaway
Offshore labor is 60-85% cheaper than US in-house, which is the bar AI must clear; BPOs are shifting to agentic and outcome-based pricing and some are under margin pressure. Sources are weak (vendor blogs) outside Genpact's own call.

### Cited Findings
- Cost per FTE (vendor-blog estimates, unreliable): US in-house fully loaded ~$75-100k (one: $77.3k); Philippines ~$13.5-29k/yr ($1,500-2,500/month); India ~$12-18k/yr ($1,200-2,200/month); offshore saving 60-80%. F&A BPO example $15/h vs $45/h in-house. — [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/us-vs-philippines-customer-support-cost-comparison-2026); [VA Masters](https://vamasters.com/bpo-cost-per-agent-breakdown/); [HiveDesk](https://www.hivedesk.com/blog/outsourcing/bpo-pricing-guide-2026); [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/outsourcing-bpo-industry-statistics/)
- Market: India roughly 5.4M+ BPO/IT-BPM workers; Philippines ~1.9-2.0M workers, $38-42B revenue (vendor compilation; verify with NASSCOM/IBPAP). — [Stealth Agents Philippines](https://stealthagents.com/research/philippines-bpo-industry-statistics-2026)
- Genpact Q2 2026 call (via Nasdaq summary): Advanced Technology Solutions revenue $363M (+24%); Core Business Services +1.9%; expects >$1B agentic total contract value booked in 2026 (~5x 2025); clients moving to agentic delivery gave >3% net revenue growth and >300bps gross-margin expansion. — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/genpact-q2-earnings-call-highlights)
- Concentrix FQ2 2026: full-year guidance cut, clients reducing volumes or seeking renegotiated pricing, stock fell sharply (secondary). — [Kavout](https://www.kavout.com/market-lens/concentrix-s-painful-pivot-why-traditional-outsourcing-is-eroding-profitability-amidst-ai-promise)
- TaskUs: AI services segment growing faster than CX lines; thin evidence. Industry trend toward outcome-based pricing (per resolved ticket/processed invoice). — [CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/taskus-ai-services/); [Parloa](https://www.parloa.com/knowledge-hub/top-bpo-companies/)

### Inferences
- A BPO is a natural distribution channel/customer: it repeats the same portal workflows for many clients and needs margin-protecting automation. CONXA must beat ~$6-15/hour offshore labor, so value is in error/audit/availability, not raw labor arbitrage, for low-wage work.

### Gaps
- Earnings-call quotes from Accenture, TaskUs, Concentrix on what they say they cannot automate were not found. No back-office-specific market size.

## 5. Scaling without headcount (ops headcount vs revenue) and domain data

### Takeaway
Healthcare revenue cycle has the best-quantified portal-work burden; insurance and freight have strong qualitative evidence but weak hard numbers; no domain yielded a clean "ops headcount scales with revenue" study.

### Cited Findings
- Healthcare admin (CAQH Index 2025): industry avoided ~$258B via automation; remaining opportunity ~$20-21B/yr from automating manual and partially manual transactions (AJMC says $18.7B potential vs $16.4B in 2023; figures conflict, check primary); transaction volume +10% medical; medical electronic prior auth 40%. — [CAQH blog](https://www.caqh.org/blog/2025-caqh-index-shows-u.s.-healthcare-avoided-258-billion-and-accelerated-automation-interoperability-and-ai-adoption); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/caqh-index-finds-20-billion-in-cost-savings-opportunities); [CAQH Executive Report PDF](https://index.caqh.org/hubfs/2025-26_Index_Dashboard/AskTheIndex_KnowledgeBase/2025%20CAQH%20Index%20Report%20Executive%20Report.pdf)
- Prior authorization (AMA 2024 survey via secondary): ~39 PAs per physician per week; ~13 hours/physician/week physician plus staff time; manual PA transaction ~$11-13 vs ~$3.22 fully electronic (CAQH 2024, secondary). — [HMP Global](https://www.hmpglobalevents.com/tciv/news-insights/prior-authorization-burden-hidden-valuation-driver-ama-finds-39-prior-auth); [ACP toolkit](https://www.acponline.org/advocacy/state-health-policy/toolkit-addressing-the-administrative-burden-of-prior-authorization); [Nirmitee](https://nirmitee.io/blog/true-cost-prior-authorization-data-driven-analysis-cms-ama-caqh/)
- Denials: rework cost ~$57.23 per denied claim in 2023 vs $43.84 in 2022 (Premier, secondary); MGMA $25.20 avg; HFMA range $25-118; Becker's headline ~$118 per claim; 35-65% of denied claims never reworked (vendor). Initial denial rate 11.8% unverified. — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/denial-rework-costs-providers-roughly-118-per-claim-4-takeaways.html); [TempDev](https://www.tempdev.com/blog/2026/03/16/64-healthcare-denial-statistics-for-2026); [Sprypt](https://www.sprypt.com/blog/what-does-a-single-denied-claim-actually-cost)
- Insurance brokerage: Applied Systems connectivity report, 74% of agents say re-keying risk data into multiple carrier portals is unacceptable; 79% want carrier automation for commercial lines; other complaints: inconsistent underwriting questions 42%, slow portals 38%, multiple logins 37%. Only hours/week survey is 2007 (OLD). Vendor estimate 15-20 h/week of redundant entry is unsourced. — [Risk & Insurance on Ivans](https://riskandinsurance.com/re-keying-and-workflow-friction-are-costing-carriers-placement-ivans-survey-finds/); [Insurance Journal 2024](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2024/01/16/755751.htm); [Duet guide](https://duet.so/guides/how-to-cut-rekeying-in-carrier-portals)
- Freight brokerage: Trucker Tools survey - 70% of brokers use manual methods to find capacity; only 10% can state cost per load; 40% do not calculate it. Vendor model: dispatcher time per load 55-82 min to 11-21 min (TMS), $18-24 saved per load (unsourced). EDI 204/211 re-keying into TMS cited as error source. — [Trucker Tools](https://www.truckertools.com/four-key-takeaways-from-trucker-tools-broker-technology-survey/); [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/the-hidden-cost-of-manual-processes-in-freight-brokerage); [Kleinschmidt](https://www.kleinschmidtinc.com/?p=5966)
- Accounting: see section 1 (pipeline fell about one-third; 42% turning away work).

### Inferences
- Healthcare RCM is the clearest first wedge: high volume (tens of PAs/week/physician), known per-transaction cost, dozens of payer portals without APIs.
- Using the 39 PA/week and ~13 h/week figures, a 10-physician practice spends ~130 h/week (~3 FTE) on PA alone (my arithmetic from secondary numbers).

### Gaps
- No evidence found on ops headcount-vs-revenue ratios in customer onboarding/implementation, claims, e-commerce marketplace ops, payroll, or compliance filings; no verified MGMA/HFMA primary reports opened.

## 6. Regulated audit needs: value of deterministic, versioned, reviewable procedures

### Takeaway
Auditors' core objection to LLM agents is non-reproducibility; the emerging consensus (mostly vendor-sourced) is "AI at build time, deterministic validated code at run time," which is CONXA's architecture. Regulatory text itself was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- 21 CFR Part 11: audit trails must be secure, computer-generated, time-stamped and must not obscure prior entries (§11.10(e), via checklist summary); validation of systems required. — [LIMSWiki checklist](https://limswiki.org/index.php/21_CFR_Part_11/Audit_guidelines_and_checklist); [MasterControl](https://www.mastercontrol.com/glossary-page/21-cfr-part-11/)
- Vendor claim (unverified against fda.gov): FDA Computer Software Assurance guidance Sept 2025 moves to risk-based validation. — [Kognitos](https://www.kognitos.com/blog/21-cfr-part-11/)
- SOX/COSO: FloQast reports COSO issued gen-AI internal-control guidance Feb 2026; audit trail expected to include prompts, inputs, outputs, model/config versions, human review evidence; recommends AI at build time then validated, version-tied deterministic code per cycle with maker/checker. Slash: same agent may not follow same path twice, undermining proof of what and why. Academic papers argue non-deterministic agent logic could weaken SOX 302/404 certification. — [FloQast](https://www.floqast.com/blog/what-ai-audit-controls-actually-look-like); [Slash](https://www.slash.com/blog/agents-and-audit-trails); [Kognitos SOX](https://www.kognitos.com/blog/sox-auditor-questions-ai-automation/); [Synapse paper](https://synapsesocial.com/papers/6a1a816c0307b785094334cd)

### Inferences
- A compiled, versioned skill package with human review (Human Review gate) maps onto change-management and evidence needs (what ran, which version, who approved). An improvising agent offers none of these. This is an inference, not an auditor-confirmed position.
- HIPAA was not researched; no source retrieved.

### Gaps
- No PCAOB/SEC/FDA primary text; HIPAA audit-control requirements; no auditor interviews. COSO Feb 2026 claim is from a single vendor blog.

## 7. Ten-plus candidate domains (browser work across disconnected portals)

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings (domain; evidence level)
1. Healthcare prior authorization / payer portals - ~39 PAs/physician/week, ~13 h/week (AMA 2024 via secondary), $11-13 per manual transaction. Sources in section 5.

7. Utilities back office and field-support systems - large retirement exposure (148k aged 55-64). Section 1.
8. Pharma/life-sciences GxP procedures (Part 11) - validation and audit-trail need; no volume data. Section 6.
9. BPO providers themselves (repeated client-portal tasks, outcome-based pricing) - Genpact agentic TCV >$1B. Section 4.
10. SOX finance close and control execution (reconciliations, evidence pulls across ERPs/banks) - audit reproducibility value; no volume data. Section 6.
11. Accounts payable invoice portal entry - $53.50 per invoice error (IOFM via vendor), 1.6-2% AP error rate. Section 3.

### Inferences (unquantified candidates for the report writer to validate, not sourced)
